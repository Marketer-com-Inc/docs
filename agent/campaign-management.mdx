Campaign management in Marketer means more than scheduling posts or uploading creatives into Ads Manager. The agent takes end-to-end ownership of your campaigns across Meta, Google, Snapchat, and ChatGPT Ads — launching new campaigns based on your briefs, pausing ad sets when they stop delivering, reallocating budget to what's working, and cleaning the keyword list so wasted spend doesn't quietly compound. Every action is logged, every material change requires your sign-off, and nothing irreversible happens without your approval.

## What the Agent Can Do

The agent covers the full spectrum of campaign operations, from initial build to ongoing optimization.

<Accordion title="Launch new campaigns and ad sets">
  When you brief the agent on a new product, angle, or seasonal push, it builds the campaign structure — naming conventions, audience targeting, ad set configuration — and queues it for your review before anything goes live. Launches follow your account's existing conventions automatically so reporting holds up from day one.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Pause underperforming ad sets">
  When ROAS, CTR, or spend efficiency on an ad set drops below threshold, the agent pauses it and logs the reason in the Campaign Activity panel. You see exactly which ad set was paused, the metric that triggered it, and the timestamp — so there's no guesswork in your weekly review.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Shift budget between campaigns and ad sets">
  The agent continuously evaluates budget allocation against live performance. When one campaign or ad set is outpacing its peers, it proposes — or within pre-approved parameters, executes — a budget shift to capture the upside before it fades.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Clean negative keywords (Google)">
  Negative keyword hygiene is one of the highest-leverage and most neglected tasks in Google Ads management. The agent audits your keyword lists regularly, flags irrelevant or budget-draining terms, and removes them — keeping Quality Score healthy and wasted spend in check.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Push new creatives live">
  Upload your creative assets and the agent handles the rest: format compliance checks against platform specs, naming convention enforcement, and direct push to Meta and Google. No manual trafficking, no spec-check spreadsheets.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Consolidate campaigns">
  Over time, ad accounts accumulate redundant campaigns, fragmented audiences, and overlapping ad sets that reduce delivery efficiency. The agent identifies consolidation opportunities and surfaces them for your approval — cleaning up account structure without disrupting active performance.
</Accordion>

## Approval Flow

The agent queues every material action before executing it. You review proposed actions in the Campaign Activity panel or directly in Slack, then approve, edit, or reject each one.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Agent detects a signal">
    Performance data crosses a threshold — a ROAS drop, a budget pacing issue, a creative going stale. The agent evaluates the signal and generates a recommended action.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Action surfaces for review">
    The proposed action appears in the Campaign Activity panel under **Needs Review**, with full context: what triggered it, what the agent wants to do, and the expected impact.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You approve, edit, or reject">
    Review the action in the dashboard or directly in Slack. Approve it to execute immediately, edit the parameters if you want to modify the action, or reject it if you disagree with the recommendation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Action executes and logs">
    Approved actions execute against the live ad account instantly. Every action — approved, edited, or rejected — is logged in the Campaign Activity panel with a timestamp and the decision made.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  By default, every material change — significant budget shifts, new campaign launches, pausing a top-line ad set — requires your explicit approval before the agent executes it. The agent never makes a high-stakes decision unilaterally.
</Note>

<Tip>
  Experienced teams running high-volume accounts can enable **auto-approve mode** for specific action types — for example, automatically approving negative keyword removals or budget micro-shifts within a defined range. This lets the agent move faster on high-confidence, lower-stakes optimizations without adding friction to your review queue.
</Tip>

## Bulk Creative Uploads

If you're a media buyer managing high creative volume, Marketer supports bulk creative uploads with direct push to Meta and Google.

- Upload multiple assets at once — images, videos, and copy variants
- Format and spec compliance is checked automatically against each platform's current requirements
- Naming conventions are enforced at upload so your reporting stays clean and filterable
- Assets are organized by campaign, ad set, and audience segment so nothing gets misrouted

<Info>
  Marketer checks every uploaded asset against live platform specs — dimensions, file size, aspect ratio, text overlay limits — before pushing. Assets that fail a spec check are flagged for correction before they're submitted, not after they've been rejected by the platform.
</Info>

## Budget Allocation by Audience Segment

The agent manages budget allocation across three core audience segments, which you configure in your account settings:

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="New-to-Brand">
    Cold prospecting audiences who haven't interacted with your brand before. The agent monitors acquisition efficiency — CPM, CTR, and cost per new customer — and adjusts spend based on how well prospecting campaigns are converting.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Engaged">
    Audiences who have interacted with your brand — site visitors, video viewers, social engagers — but haven't converted. The agent watches retargeting ROAS closely and reallocates when engagement audiences start to saturate.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Existing Customers">
    Your existing customer base, targeted for repeat purchase, upsell, or LTV expansion. The agent tracks incremental ROAS on customer campaigns separately so you can see the true return on retention spend.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>
