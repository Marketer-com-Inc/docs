Marketer gives you a single place to see what your ad spend is actually producing — not vanity metrics in disconnected platform dashboards, but revenue-tied attribution across every channel, audience segment, and creative angle you're running. The performance layer isn't just a reporting surface: it feeds directly into the agent's decision-making, so when you see a metric shift in the Quick Insights panel, the agent has already evaluated it and either acted or queued a recommendation. You're not reading stale data and deciding what to do next — Marketer has already started moving.

## Attribution

Platform-reported ROAS is not the whole story. Marketer's attribution layer connects your ad spend data to actual revenue — pulling in store data and Klaviyo alongside platform signals — so you have a single source of truth that holds up under scrutiny.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Revenue-Tied Attribution">
    Every campaign, ad set, and creative is attributed against real revenue, not just platform-reported conversions. This means you can see which channels and angles are actually driving incremental revenue — and which ones are claiming credit for sales that would have happened anyway.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Cross-Channel View">
    Marketer aggregates attribution across Meta, Google, Snapchat, and email (Klaviyo) in a single dashboard. You stop having to reconcile five different platform reports and start making decisions from one unified number.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Board-Ready Reporting">
    The attribution dashboard is structured for executive and board-level reporting — clear revenue contribution by channel, ROAS by audience segment, and period-over-period comparisons that tell a coherent story without requiring you to build a deck from scratch.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Creative Fatigue Detection

One of the most common sources of silent budget waste in performance marketing is running creative past its effective lifespan. Frequency climbs, CTR slides, and ROAS erodes — often over days before anyone notices and pulls the asset.

Marketer watches for fatigue signals continuously. When a creative starts underperforming relative to its own baseline — not just against account averages — the agent flags it in the Quick Insights panel and queues a recommendation: pause the asset, rotate in a fresh variant, or test a new angle.

<Warning>
  Ignoring creative fatigue signals typically means continuing to pay rising CPMs for declining returns. The agent flags these early — act on them before spend compounds against a creative that's already lost its edge.
</Warning>

## Audience Insights

Marketer tracks performance not just by campaign and ad set, but by creative angle — so you can see which messaging frameworks are actually moving your audience.

<Accordion title="Ingredient / Product Angle">
  Performance data for ads leading with product features, formulation, or ingredient-level claims. Useful for understanding how much of your audience is in a research-and-compare mindset versus an emotional-purchase mindset.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Lifestyle Angle">
  Performance data for ads that lead with aspiration, identity, or use-case imagery rather than product specifics. Often the highest-reach angle for new-to-brand audiences.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="UGC Angle">
  Performance data for user-generated or UGC-style content — reviews, testimonials, unboxings, and creator-style videos. Marketer tracks whether UGC is outperforming produced creative in your account and flags when the gap is significant enough to shift budget toward it.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Founder / Story Angle">
  Performance data for founder-led or brand-story content. Particularly valuable for DTC brands where founder narrative is a differentiator — Marketer tracks whether this angle holds its ROAS over time or is best used in specific funnel stages.
</Accordion>

## Research Engine

Performance monitoring in Marketer isn't limited to what's happening inside your ad accounts. The research engine pulls external signals continuously — so when your account data shifts, there's often context available for why.

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="Reddit Signals" icon="reddit">
    Trending conversations in your category — product questions, ingredient concerns, competitor comparisons — surfaced as context for your creative and messaging decisions.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Google Trends" icon="magnifying-glass-chart">
    Search demand shifts for your core terms and category keywords, updated continuously so you can see if a ROAS drop is an account issue or a broader demand signal.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Competitor Insights" icon="binoculars">
    A continuously updated feed of competitor creative activity — new ad formats, angles, and offers — so you're not flying blind when the competitive landscape shifts.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Info>
  Your data stays yours. Marketer reads your ad account data and store data to power performance monitoring and attribution, but your data is never transferred to or shared with third parties. The research engine pulls publicly available signals — Reddit, Google Trends, ad library data — and surfaces them as context, not as inputs that touch your account data.
</Info>
