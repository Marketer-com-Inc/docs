Huckle Bee Farms is a US direct-to-consumer honey brand with 40+ SKUs. When founder Jim Douglas came to Marketer, his Shopify revenue for the full prior year had been $435K. Honey is one of the hardest DTC categories to acquire on paid: low average order value, narrow buyer intent, and a repeat-purchase model that only pays off if you reach the right buyer in the first place. Getting there had been the problem.

## The Challenge

Jim had already been through two agencies. Each took the budget, ran the campaigns, and came back with reports. When the numbers didn't move, the answer was always a version of the same thing: trust the algorithm, give it more time. Few new customers. No real growth. Just spend and explanations.

Jim came to Marketer looking for a different deal: a team with skin in the game.

## The Solution

The engagement runs on a revenue-share structure — Marketer only makes money if Huckle Bee does. One team replaced two agencies. Campaign architecture was rebuilt from scratch.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Net-New Customer Acquisition on Meta">
    New customer acquisition campaigns were built from the ground up on Meta, with fresh creative refreshed weekly and pixel events validated end to end.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Spend Scaling with Efficiency Held">
    Ad spend scaled from $14K to $295K with efficiency maintained throughout. Aggregate marketing efficiency ratio (aMER) held above 2.7× through a 20× increase in spend.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Unit Economics Protected">
    As spend scaled, the campaign architecture was managed to protect unit economics — ensuring the growth in volume didn't come at the cost of profitability per customer.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Key Takeaways

<Tip>
  For food and DTC brands with low average order value, the acquisition model matters as much as the channel. Huckle Bee Farms' turnaround came from rebuilding campaign architecture with a performance-aligned team, not from incremental optimisation on top of a broken structure. The revenue-share model aligned incentives — and the results followed.
</Tip>
