SoftMoc is a Canadian footwear institution. Family-owned since 1990, the retailer operates 135+ stores across the country and generates more than $350M in annual revenue. They were also among North America's earliest online retailers — selling on the web since 1997, well before e-commerce was a default assumption for any brand. By any measure, SoftMoc knows how to sell shoes. What they didn't have was a creative infrastructure that could match the scale of their catalog, and that gap was quietly costing them one of their highest-performing channels.

## The Challenge

SoftMoc's Meta ads were performing well. That was the problem hiding in plain sight.

Despite strong returns, Meta was only receiving 20% of SoftMoc's total advertising budget — not because the channel was underperforming, but because the creative pipeline couldn't keep up with a catalog of 3,000+ SKUs spanning 25+ best-selling footwear brands. Every time the team tried to scale, they ran into the same wall: not enough creatives to properly cover the catalog, test new angles, or give Meta's algorithm the volume of inputs it needs to optimise efficiently.

As one stakeholder put it, SoftMoc was "underserved in a huge way" by their own creative capacity. The channel had more room to grow than almost anything else in the budget — it just needed a system that could actually feed it.

## The Solution

Marketer approached SoftMoc's problem as an infrastructure challenge, not a campaign challenge. The goal was to build the creative foundation that would allow Meta to do what it does best.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Industrial Creative Production">
    Marketer produced **536 creatives in 10 weeks**, achieving SKU-level coverage across SoftMoc's 25+ brands. For the first time, the catalog had a creative asset for nearly every product that needed one — removing the bottleneck that had kept Meta underfunded.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Video vs. Static Testing">
    With creative volume in place, Marketer ran a structured test to determine which format was driving real incremental returns. Video won clearly: video ads delivered a **5.43× incremental ROAS** compared to **4.31× for static**. Future production weighted toward video accordingly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="French Quebec Localization">
    Starting from the third creative batch, Marketer added French-language Quebec localization to the production pipeline. Canada's French-speaking population represents a meaningful share of the addressable market — and serving them in their language expanded the audience SoftMoc could reach efficiently on Meta.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Single Acquisition Campaign Consolidation">
    Rather than running fragmented campaigns by brand or product category, Marketer consolidated SoftMoc's Meta activity under a single new customer acquisition campaign. This gave the algorithm a unified signal and a larger pool of creative to optimise across — the conditions it needs to find the right audience at the right cost.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Meta Conversion Lift Study">
    To measure the program's true impact, Marketer built a Meta Conversion Lift study designed to withstand scrutiny from SoftMoc's finance team — not just marketing dashboards. The study produced incremental attribution data that demonstrated the channel's contribution in terms that hold up to rigorous internal review.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Key Takeaways

<Tip>
  **Two quick wins for catalog-heavy retailers:** First, run a structured video vs. static test — the format difference may be worth more than any targeting adjustment. Second, if you sell in a bilingual or multilingual market, localization is an underutilised lever. SoftMoc's Quebec French expansion grew their addressable Meta audience at minimal additional creative cost, because the production infrastructure was already in place to absorb it.
</Tip>
