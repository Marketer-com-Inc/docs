The brief is where every Ember creative begins — and getting it right is simpler than you might expect. Unlike a traditional agency engagement, there's no portfolio review, no scoping call, no quote to approve, and no shared Slack channel to manage. You submit your direction inside the Marketer platform, Ember's production pipeline runs, senior designers review the output, and finished creatives land in your dashboard ready to push live. The workflow is designed to eliminate the coordination overhead that makes creative production slow — and to feed performance data back into the next brief automatically so the cycle compounds over time.

## The Briefing Workflow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Brand Onboarding">
    The first time you connect to Ember, the system indexes your brand. This isn't a questionnaire — Ember pulls directly from your existing ad accounts and data sources to build your brand profile from evidence, not assumptions.

    During onboarding, Ember:
    - **Fetches your campaigns** — pulling creative history, campaign structure, and performance data from your connected ad accounts
    - **Indexes your creatives** — building a searchable map of every asset you've run, tagged by format, angle, and performance signal
    - **Syncs your analytics, audiences, and product catalog** — so every brief can be targeted to a specific audience segment and product set

    This foundation is what powers Ember's On-Brand Guarantee and its ability to route performance signals back into future briefs. The richer your historical data, the stronger the starting point.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Submit a Brief">
    Inside the Marketer platform, submit a creative brief directly to Ember. A brief covers:

    - **Format** — which of the six creative types you need (Ad Creative, Video, Product Shot, AI UGC, Web Creative, Email & SMS)
    - **Creative angle** — the strategic direction (e.g. ingredient-forward, lifestyle, founder story, promotional offer, social proof)
    - **Audience segment** — who this creative is targeting, drawn from your synced audience data
    - **Platform and placement** — where the creative will run, so spec compliance is handled automatically
    - **Additional context** — any campaign-specific details, seasonal considerations, or creative references

    Ember's AI agent handles the briefing interface — you describe direction, and the agent structures it into the production pipeline. No brief template to fill out manually.

    <Note>
      If you're using Ember without the full Marketer platform, you submit briefs through the Ember dashboard directly. The workflow is the same — the connection to Marketer's campaign management and media buying features simply isn't active.
    </Note>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Ember Produces">
    Once your brief is submitted, Ember's 5-step image generation pipeline runs:

    <Accordion title="View the 5-step production pipeline">
      1. **Subject** — The primary subject of the creative is defined and rendered: product, person, or scene, depending on format
      2. **Style** — Visual style is applied based on your brand guidelines, the target platform, and the creative angle in your brief
      3. **World / Background** — The environment and context are constructed around the subject — location, setting, atmosphere
      4. **Lighting** — Lighting is calibrated for the format and placement: studio-clean for product shots, naturalistic for UGC, high-contrast for performance static
      5. **Final Details** — Typography, copy, CTA, logo placement, and finishing details are applied and checked against your brand guidelines

      Senior designers review and curate the output at the end of this pipeline before anything reaches your dashboard. You're not seeing raw model output — you're seeing production-ready creative.
    </Accordion>

    <Info>
      **Turnaround time is 3–7 days for most formats.** Complex multi-format batches or localization requests may extend toward the higher end of that range. Your dashboard shows live production status for each brief in progress.
    </Info>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Review and Approve">
    Finished concepts appear in your Ember dashboard for review. You can:

    - **Approve** individual assets to move them to the push queue
    - **Request revisions** with specific direction — the agent handles routing back into production
    - **Compare variants** side by side before deciding which to push live

    <Note>
      **On-Brand Guarantee:** Every Ember output is automatically checked against your indexed brand guidelines before it reaches your dashboard. If an asset doesn't pass the brand compliance check, it's revised in production — you won't see outputs that violate your brand standards. The guarantee applies to every format, every batch.
    </Note>

    You don't need to check logos, color values, or typography manually. That review happens before your dashboard ever shows you the creative.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Push Live">
    Approved creatives are pushed directly to your ad accounts from within Marketer. No CSV export, no copy-paste into Ads Manager, no manual asset upload.

    - Push to **Meta** (Facebook and Instagram ad accounts)
    - Push to **Google** (Performance Max, Display, Shopping)
    - Push to **Snapchat** (directly to your Snap Ads Manager)

    Once live, performance data from these creatives feeds back into Ember automatically — so the next brief starts with signal, not a blank page.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What Happens Next

Pushing a creative live isn't the end of the workflow — it's the beginning of the next one. Ember reads performance data from every asset you run and surfaces winning signals back into your briefing process automatically.

<Tip>
  You don't need to manually analyze which creative angles are working. Ember's performance loop identifies winners, flags creative fatigue, and updates the intelligence behind your next brief without any manual input from your team. See [Performance Loop](/ember/performance-loop) for how this works.
</Tip>

The result is a closed system: brief, produce, push, learn, brief again — with each cycle building on the last instead of starting from scratch.
