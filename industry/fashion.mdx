Fashion's drop cadence has outrun the traditional agency model entirely. Trend cycles now move in days, capsule collections land on weeks-long runways, and the brands winning on paid social are the ones who can brief, produce, launch, and iterate inside a single week — not a single quarter. A 6-week agency turnaround doesn't just feel slow in fashion; it's a structural disadvantage that hands the moment to whoever ships fastest.

## How Marketer Solves It

Marketer turns each drop into a repeatable system. The moment a brief is set, Ember generates static, video, and motion creative at the volume a launch actually demands — not one hero asset, but a full batch built to cover Meta, Google, and Snapchat simultaneously. Creative goes from brief to live without the back-and-forth that kills launch timing. For capsule collections and core range alike, you get the coverage to test multiple angles and concentrate spend on what's converting before the drop window closes.

The AI Agent runs continuously across your active campaigns, watching for the two problems that quietly end a profitable drop week: creative fatigue and budget leaks. When an ad set starts to tire mid-week — before it shows up in a Monday morning report — the Agent flags it and recommends action. You don't lose the back half of a launch to an ad that should have rotated on Wednesday.

Klaviyo drop announcements run from the same source of truth as your paid campaigns, so your email list and your ad audiences hear the same message at the same moment. When performance data comes back from the first 48 hours, it feeds directly into the next drop brief through Marketer's performance loop — each collection benefits from everything you learned in the last one.
