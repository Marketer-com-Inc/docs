When you connect Meta to Marketer, your AI agent gains a live read of every campaign, ad set, creative, and audience in your account — along with the pixel events that tell the real performance story. From that foundation, the agent can surface what's working, flag what's draining budget, and take targeted action: pausing underperformers, shifting spend to what's converting, launching new ad sets, and keeping Meta fed with fresh creative. Every material action comes back to you for approval before it executes, so you stay in control without staying in the weeds.

## How to Connect Meta

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Settings → Integrations">
    In Marketer, navigate to **Settings** in the left sidebar and select **Integrations** from the menu.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Click 'Connect Meta'">
    Find the Meta card in your integrations list and click **Connect Meta** to begin the authorisation flow.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Log in and grant access">
    You'll be redirected to Meta. Log in to your Meta Business account and grant Marketer the requested permissions. Marketer requests read and write access to manage ads on your behalf.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select your ad accounts">
    After granting access, select which Meta ad accounts you want to connect to Marketer. You can connect multiple accounts if you manage more than one.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Marketer indexes your account">
    Marketer immediately begins indexing your campaigns, ad sets, creatives, audiences, and pixel data. Depending on account size, initial indexing takes a few minutes. Once complete, your agent is ready to work.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What Marketer Reads from Meta

Marketer pulls the following data from your connected Meta ad accounts:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Campaigns" icon="flag">
    Campaign names, objectives, status, budgets, and top-level performance metrics.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Ad Sets" icon="layer-group">
    Ad set structure, targeting parameters, scheduling, spend, and delivery data.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Creatives" icon="image">
    Ad creative assets, copy, formats, and creative-level performance.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Audiences" icon="users">
    Custom audiences, lookalike audiences, and saved audience definitions.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Pixel Events" icon="bolt">
    Purchase events, add-to-cart, initiate checkout, and custom conversion signals from your Meta pixel.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Performance Data" icon="chart-line">
    Impressions, reach, clicks, spend, ROAS, CPM, CPC, and conversion data at every level of the account.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## What the Agent Can Do on Meta

Once connected, your Marketer agent can take the following actions — each surfaced for your approval before executing:

- **Pause underperforming ad sets** based on spend thresholds and conversion data
- **Shift budget** between ad sets and campaigns to follow what's converting
- **Launch new campaigns and ad sets** built from your briefs and product data
- **Push new creatives** into live ad sets to refresh frequency and feed the algorithm
- **Adjust audiences** — swap in updated custom audiences or test new lookalike segments

<Note>
  Marketer surfaces every material action — budget changes, pauses, launches, creative pushes — for your review before executing. You approve the plan; the agent handles the execution. You can also configure auto-approval thresholds for routine optimisations if you prefer a more hands-off workflow.
</Note>

<Tip>
  Meta's algorithm rewards accounts that consistently receive fresh creative. Use **Ember**, Marketer's creative engine, to maintain the cadence Meta wants without your team burning time on production. Ember can generate new ad variants, static assets, and video concepts briefed directly from your campaign data — keeping your creative pipeline moving automatically.
</Tip>
