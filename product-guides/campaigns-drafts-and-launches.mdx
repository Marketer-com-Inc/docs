Campaign work can exist as an editable Marketer draft, an in-progress launch, or live provider objects. **Drafts** and **Launch ads** keep those states distinct.

Open [Campaigns](https://app.marketer.com/dashboard/campaigns), then choose **Drafts** or **Launch ads**.

<Frame caption="Drafts separates work you can still edit from publishing work that must be followed through launch progress.">
  <img src="/assets/product-flows/campaigns-drafts.png" alt="Campaigns Drafts drawer with Meta and Google drafts, Review actions, and a publishing campaign with View progress" />
</Frame>

## Read the state before choosing an action

| State | Correct action | What happens next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Draft or editing | **Review** | The saved provider-specific builder opens |
| Publishing | **View progress** | The per-object launch ledger opens |
| Completed | Open the resulting campaign | Verify hierarchy and provider status |
| Partial or failed | **View progress**, then inspect failed objects | Repair only the unresolved part before retry |

Do not start a new campaign just because a launch is slow. A second submission can create duplicate campaigns, child objects, or ads while the first is still resolving.

## Resume an editable draft

<Steps>
  <Step title="Identify the exact draft">
    Confirm provider, draft name, last-updated state, and campaign type where shown. Similar names are not interchangeable.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open Review">
    Select **Review** to resume the stored builder. A Google draft returns to its stored type; a Meta draft returns to its saved campaign tree.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check for stale inputs">
    Reverify account access, budget, dates, destination, product availability, audience, conversion, and creative. A draft can remain valid structurally while its business inputs become stale.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Resolve validation">
    Open each named blocker and correct the owning object. Do not recreate the draft to bypass a specific validation message.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish only after a fresh review">
    Confirm the final object counts and target state, then approve the provider submission.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Use the row menu to rename or duplicate only when those actions are shown. Reopen the resulting record and confirm it is a distinct draft before changing it.

Deleting a draft removes that in-progress Marketer record. It does not delete an unrelated provider campaign. Confirm identity and state before the delete confirmation.

## Interpret launch progress

A launch can create several provider objects. Read progress at the campaign, child, and ad levels rather than relying on the top summary alone.

For each expected object, record:

- Intended object and parent
- Current launch state
- Provider identifier when created
- Failure or warning text
- Whether a retry is offered
- Final provider status

The launch is complete only when every expected object has a terminal result and the successful objects appear under the intended parent.

## Recover from a partial launch

<Steps>
  <Step title="Freeze new submissions">
    Do not publish the campaign again while the original launch can still progress.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Inventory successful objects">
    Use **View progress**, Campaigns, and the provider account to identify exactly what already exists.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the failed object's reason">
    Locate the validation, authorization, provider, asset, or destination failure at the lowest failed object.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Repair the owning input">
    Correct only the failed configuration or connection. Preserve provider identifiers for every successful object.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use the offered retry path">
    Retry only after the original state is terminal and the product exposes a scoped recovery action. If the UI proposes a broad re-publication, verify it will not duplicate successful objects before confirmation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify parentage and state">
    Confirm the recovered child or ad belongs to the intended campaign and reaches the planned initial status.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Completion check

- The resumed record is the intended provider and draft.
- Business inputs were revalidated after the last edit.
- Review shows the expected campaign, child, and ad count.
- Every launch object reached a terminal state.
- Successful provider objects and their parentage are recorded.
- No duplicate campaign, child, or ad was created during recovery.
- Campaigns and the provider show the expected post-launch status.

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="A draft opens the wrong builder">
    Return to **Drafts** and confirm provider, type, and name. A stored Google draft intentionally resumes its original type; create a new draft if the campaign type truly needs to change.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A launch appears stuck">
    Open **View progress**, inspect the lowest unresolved object, and check provider authorization and task activity. Avoid repeating publish while the run remains non-terminal.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Some provider objects exist and others failed">
    Treat the launch as partial. Inventory the successful identifiers, fix the exact failed input, and use the scoped retry only after confirming it preserves successful objects.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Campaigns does not show a successful object yet">
    Verify it in the provider, then wait for the connected account to sync. Keep the launch record and provider identifier; do not recreate the object to make the table update faster.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The launch needs approval">
    Open **Awaiting action**, compare the exact target, old and new state, budget, schedule, and risk flags, then return to **View progress** after the decision.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
