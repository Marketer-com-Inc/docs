**Knowledge** and **Skills** help Marketer carry useful context from one task to the next. They serve different purposes:

- **Knowledge** holds facts, source material, brand context, and artifacts produced by work.
- **Skills** hold reusable procedures that tell Marketer how to approach recurring kinds of work.

Use Knowledge for what is true. Use Skills for how work should be done.

## Knowledge

The Knowledge overview helps you inspect the context available to the workspace. It can surface approved source material, the standing brand context, and recent artifacts created by Marketer.

Use Knowledge when you need to:

- Review the material that grounds brand, product, audience, or strategy work.
- Add or update an approved source.
- Inspect a recent research or analysis artifact.
- Check whether an agent result is grounded in the expected source.
- Find missing or conflicting workspace context before starting new work.

<Note>
  Knowledge improves future work only when its source and scope are clear. A fact without provenance, date, or ownership can become a confident source of error.
</Note>

## Skills

A Skill is a reusable way of working. It is most useful for a recurring, procedural need such as a campaign naming convention, a pre-launch review checklist, or the steps your team follows before changing a budget.

The Skills surface separates active procedures from suggestions that still need review. It also gives you a place to inspect existing Skills before you add another one.

A good Skill explains:

- When it should be used.
- When it should not be used.
- The ordered steps to follow.
- The evidence or inputs each step requires.
- The decision rules and stopping conditions.
- The final self-check.

Do not use a Skill as a container for a volatile price, current campaign result, or one-time request. Put stable facts in Knowledge and fetch current operating data from the surface that owns it.

## Add grounded knowledge

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose the source">
    Use an approved source that belongs in the workspace. Avoid uploading duplicates or material that the whole workspace should not rely on.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Define its scope">
    Record what the source covers, who owns it, and when it was last verified. If a source is historical, make that boundary visible.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Resolve conflicts">
    When two sources disagree, identify the current authority instead of keeping both as equally valid instructions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Test retrieval">
    Ask the Marketing Agent a question the source should answer and require it to identify the evidence. Correct the source or scope if the result is ambiguous.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Create or review a Skill

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check for an existing procedure">
    Search the active and suggested library first. Update a relevant Skill instead of creating a near-duplicate.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Define the trigger">
    Describe the real requests or situations that should use the Skill. A vague trigger makes the procedure appear at the wrong time or not at all.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write executable steps">
    Keep the Skill focused on one recurring outcome. Name required inputs, decision rules, guardrails, and the final verification.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review its scope">
    Confirm which work or specialist should use it and whether it contains secrets, unsupported claims, or volatile facts.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Promote deliberately">
    Review a suggested Skill before making it active. Confirm that it is distinct, useful, and safe. Suggested procedures should not become workspace policy only because an agent proposed them.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Common outcomes

### Ground a creative brief

Keep approved brand voice, product facts, and audience evidence in Knowledge. Put the repeatable brief-review process in a Skill. The brief can then use current facts while following a consistent method.

### Standardize campaign QA

Create a Skill for the exact checks your team performs before launch. Link each check to the current source of truth rather than copying volatile settings into the procedure.

### Preserve research

Review a completed research artifact in Knowledge, retain the supported conclusions, and record its date and scope. If the work produced a repeatable method, save that method separately as a Skill.

### Improve a recurring process

When a procedure repeatedly produces weak results, revise its trigger, steps, or evidence requirements. Do not solve process quality by adding more overlapping Skills.

## Governance checklist

- Every important fact has an identifiable source and owner.
- Time-sensitive material includes a last-verified date or clear historical scope.
- Conflicting sources have an explicit authority order.
- Skills describe procedures, not secrets or unstable facts.
- Suggested Skills receive human review before promotion.
- Retired procedures are archived or replaced instead of silently duplicated.
- High-risk procedures include a human approval and verification step.

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The agent ignores an important fact">
    Confirm that the fact exists in the current workspace Knowledge, comes from the intended source, and is not contradicted by a higher-authority source. Attach the source or restate the constraint for the current task while you correct the workspace context.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The agent uses stale information">
    Update or remove the outdated source and record the new verification date. Re-run decision-grade work after the knowledge state is corrected.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A Skill is not being used when expected">
    Review its description and trigger language. Test it against several real requests that should and should not match, then make the boundary more specific.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A suggested Skill overlaps an active one">
    Merge the useful addition into the active procedure or reject the suggestion. Two competing instructions for the same work make future behavior less predictable.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="An artifact exists but its evidence is unclear">
    Open the artifact detail and inspect its source relationships. Treat unsupported conclusions as unresolved, then rerun the task with an explicit evidence requirement if needed.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
