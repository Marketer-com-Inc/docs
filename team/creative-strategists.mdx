The real problem in creative strategy isn't ideation — it's the time spent getting ready to ideate. Scanning competitor feeds, pulling performance reports, combing through trend data, rebuilding the ads library from screenshots: these are the tasks that fill the calendar and shrink the space for actual strategic thinking. Marketer runs the research layer continuously in the background so you arrive at every brief already informed. Cultural signals, competitive intelligence, and performance data are always current, always indexed, and always waiting when you need them. You stop gathering. You start directing.

## What Marketer Runs for You, Continuously

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Cultural Research" icon="globe">
    Marketer tracks trending topics, emerging audience signals, and cultural moments relevant to your category — updated continuously so your creative angles reflect what's actually happening, not what was relevant last quarter.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Competitive Research" icon="binoculars">
    Every competitor ad that enters the feed is captured, indexed, and made searchable. No more screenshot folders. No more manual trawls through the Meta Ads Library. The whole competitive landscape lives in one searchable index.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Performance Signals" icon="signal">
    See which creative angles are converting — ingredient-led, lifestyle, UGC, founder, problem/solution — across your accounts and the broader category. Strategy stays grounded in what's working, not what feels right.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Brief AI" icon="brain">
    Before any brief goes to production, the AI agent interrogates it against live research data. It surfaces angles worth exploring, flags assumptions that don't hold up in the data, and challenges the brief so your team walks into kickoff with stronger direction.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Tip>
  Brief AI works like a strategist who has read everything before the meeting. Feed it your brief and it comes back with specific angles — pulled from live competitive data, performance signals, and audience research — that challenge your assumptions and sharpen your direction before anyone walks into the kickoff. Use it to pressure-test the brief, not just validate it.
</Tip>

## The Research-to-Production Workflow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Research Engine Surfaces Angles">
    Marketer continuously ingests cultural, competitive, and audience data. When you open a brief, a curated set of angles — grounded in real signals — is already waiting for your review.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You Choose the Direction">
    Review the angles, apply your strategic judgement, and pick the direction worth testing. The research is already done — your job is to decide what matters.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit the Ember Production Brief">
    Brief Ember directly from the platform. Your chosen angles, format requirements, and audience context are packaged into a production brief that designers can execute against immediately.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Designers Ship the Creative">
    Ember's designers produce the volume at the cadence you set — static, video, UGC-style, motion — inside your brand parameters. Every output is curated, not just generated.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Performance Feeds Back into the Next Brief">
    Winning creative data flows back into the research engine. The next brief you write starts with better intelligence than the last one. The strategy compounds over time.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Tools Built for the Strategist's Workflow

<Accordion title="Research engine — cultural, competitive, audience">
  Marketer runs research across three tracks simultaneously. Cultural intelligence tracks what's resonating in the broader conversation. Competitive intelligence indexes every ad your competitors are running. Audience signals show you what's driving action inside the category. All three update continuously — you never work from stale data.

<Accordion title="Funnel map">

No spreadsheet reconstruction before the client call.
</Accordion>

  The full competitive feed is indexed and searchable by brand, format, angle, and platform. Stop hunting through screenshots. Find the reference you need, see when it ran, and understand the context it appeared in.
