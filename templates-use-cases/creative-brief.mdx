Use this pattern to create a clear starting brief before generating or commissioning assets. A useful brief connects the marketing goal to an audience insight and defines what the finished creative must communicate.

## Before you start

- Choose the product or offer.
- State the campaign objective and target audience.
- Add approved brand facts, voice guidance, and any required claims.
- Attach relevant performance analysis, customer research, or existing creative.
- Note format, placement, deadline, and legal or brand constraints.

## Starting request

```text
Create a creative brief for [product or offer] aimed at [audience] with the goal of [objective].
Use the attached [research, performance result, or existing creative] and the workspace brand context.
Propose one primary message, three evidence-backed angles, the desired action, required assets, and format considerations for [placement].
Flag any claim that lacks approved support. Keep the brief in draft for review.
```

## Review the draft

Check that the brief:

- Names one audience and one primary outcome.
- Uses facts that are approved in your workspace.
- Distinguishes an insight from a creative idea.
- Makes the product and offer accurate.
- Defines the deliverables without prescribing every visual decision.
- Flags unsupported claims instead of polishing them into final copy.

<Tip>
  If the brief feels generic, add a specific customer tension, a useful product fact, or a performance pattern. More adjectives rarely improve it.
</Tip>
