The Marketing Agent is your main conversational entry point. It can answer directly, work from your workspace context, or coordinate a specialist when the request benefits from focused expertise or a longer process.

You do not need to translate a marketing goal into an internal workflow. Describe the outcome, evidence, and boundaries you need. Marketer can then choose an appropriate way to handle the work and show you its progress.

## When specialist work helps

Focused work is useful when the request needs more than a quick answer, such as:

- Building or refreshing brand research from approved sources.
- Investigating campaign performance and preparing a proposed next step.
- Researching audience language or creative angles.
- Turning a brief into a set of creative directions or assets.
- Running a structured account review.
- Gathering evidence from a live web source when connected data is not enough.

The specialists available to you can vary by workspace and product state. Treat the names and capabilities shown in your interface as the current source of truth.

## Start with the Marketing Agent

<Steps>
  <Step title="Describe the outcome">
    State the decision or deliverable you need. Include the intended audience, channel, time frame, and success measure.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Provide the evidence boundary">
    Attach relevant workspace records and name any approved external sources. Say which facts must come from connected data and where inference is acceptable.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set authority and review rules">
    Specify whether the work is read-only, may produce a draft, or may prepare a proposed action. Do not combine “investigate” and “apply changes” without an explicit review point.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Follow the task">
    If Marketer starts longer specialist work, use the task detail to follow its progress. Respond when it requests input or a decision.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the combined result">
    The Marketing Agent can bring specialist findings back into the conversation. Check that the final answer resolves conflicts, cites the evidence, and stays within the original boundary.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Specialist perspective and specialist task

Marketer can apply focused expertise in two ways:

| Mode | Best for | What you receive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Focused conversation | A quick opinion, critique, or explanation in one domain | A direct response in the current thread |
| Longer task | Research, production, or analysis with multiple steps | Progress, a result or artifact, and any required review point |

If you need a durable deliverable, say so. For example, ask for “a brief with three ranked angles and a review checklist,” not just “think like a creative strategist.”

## Keep coordinated work grounded

Specialization does not replace evidence. Use the same review standards you would apply to a direct response:

- Confirm that workspace facts come from the correct Knowledge sources or connected accounts.
- Confirm that performance findings use the intended dates, provider, attribution view, currency, and freshness state.
- Require external research to identify what was observed and where.
- Review generated copy and creative for unsupported claims or invented product details.
- Treat campaign changes as proposals until the applicable approval and provider state confirm otherwise.

<Warning>
  A confident specialist voice is not proof. When a claim affects spend, customer-facing content, or strategy, require the underlying evidence and verify it in the surface that owns the data.
</Warning>

## Example requests

### Performance investigation

> Investigate the attached campaign for the last 14 complete days. Use a performance marketing perspective, separate data from hypotheses, and return a ranked diagnosis with one low-risk test. Do not change the account.

### Creative direction

> Use the attached product and current brand Knowledge to develop three distinct creative directions. For each, include the audience insight, hook, visual concept, proof requirement, and reason it is different. Produce a brief only.

### Account review

> Review the connected workspace for setup, delivery, and knowledge gaps. Cite the evidence for each finding, rank fixes by impact and effort, and keep the review read-only.

## Troubleshooting

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The wrong expertise is being applied">
    Restate the outcome and the domain that should own the decision. Attach the relevant record and explain what is out of scope. Starting a new thread can help when the current conversation has accumulated unrelated context.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Specialists returned conflicting recommendations">
    Ask the Marketing Agent to reconcile them against one success metric, the same date and attribution context, and your current guardrails. Preserve material uncertainty instead of forcing a false consensus.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The task needs more information">
    Supply the missing source or constraint through the task or conversation. If the required integration is unavailable, narrow the claim to what the current evidence supports.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The result is too broad">
    Request a defined artifact, maximum number of recommendations, ranking method, and explicit next decision. A narrower deliverable is easier to review and reuse.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
